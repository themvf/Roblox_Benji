## Source and authoring decision

- `source/bluetube_clean.glb` preserves the supplied source byte-for-byte, SHA-256 `70f218923f30d12880bdff27daa157f5c125a03f22b36b561ccd58e333ab9f3a`. `source/design-reference.png` preserves the neighboring concept image. The earlier game-size optimized baseline is preserved in `source/BlueTube_OptimizedBaseline.glb`, SHA-256 `7e5508e0da0e494852897798a3873fa9b174ce482cd2e5f1854a30a2344ec5f3`.
- `appraisal.json` and `views.png` document the clean source: 176,596 triangles, 156,878 open edges, a blue chamber and side machinery visible from multiple angles. The user's screenshot and the first trial's renders showed that reducing the generated mesh left broken geometry. A finish map could not repair its silhouette or close its top gaps.
- `tools/blender/build_bluetube_clean_geometry.py` is the authoritative builder. It models a closed top cap, continuous retaining rings, smooth chamber wall, stepped base, side cabinet, door panels, screen, vents, brackets, roof ribs and fasteners. `source/GlossyBlueTube.blend` is the editable scene before export grouping. Major source details were recreated as clean geometry and named PBR material factors; the generated mesh/UV atlas were not transferred into the delivery.
- The cyan inner core is opaque and static behind a glossy alpha-blended cyan shell. Ten named materials specify blue enamel, polished steel, rubber, display and indicators. No image texture maps are required by the current GLB; see `textures/README.md`. Gloss and glass appearance must be checked in Studio.
- `tools/build_bluetube_green_core.py` creates the green internal version without changing the blue export or mesh binary. It recolors the energy core, alpha-blended glass shell and inner indicators to green. The glossy blue outer shell, clean cap, cabinet, pivot and scale stay identical. The green look is also static; it adds no actual Roblox light or particle effect.
- `tools/build_bluetube_neon_yellow_core.py` creates the yellow variant from the verified blue export while preserving the blue and green files. It colors the core, glass and inner indicators fluorescent yellow, increases the core's glTF emissive factors, and names its mesh `Tube_Neon_yellow_energy_core`. Its geometry binary is identical to the blue export. `SetupNeonYellowCore.luau` is an optional Studio Command Bar helper to set that mesh to Roblox `Enum.Material.Neon` after import. This has not been run in Studio.
- `tools/blender/build_bluetube_cracked_oozing.py` imports the verified neon-yellow GLB and replaces its continuous glass wall with a shell physically cut open on the front. It adds branching fractures, retained shards, viscous yellow-green strands and a floor pool as static geometry. The cap, base, cabinet and energy core stay from the clean candidate. `source/CrackedOozingTube.blend` keeps the editable crack and flow objects before export grouping; `tools/build_bluetube_cracked_oozing.py` reproduces the GLB, renders and local checks. There is no fluid simulation or particle effect.
- `tools/blender/build_bluetube_faint_occupant.py` imports the verified clean blue GLB and adds a flat, softly tinted head, torso, arms and legs inside the transparent chamber wall, just in front of the opaque core. The silhouette has 22% material alpha and no face or clothing details. `source/FaintOccupantTube.blend` is the editable source. `tools/build_bluetube_faint_occupant.py` backs up previous outputs, reproduces the export, renders saved-file views and checks the earlier variant hashes.
- `tools/build_bluetube_blue_cracked_oozing.py` derives the blue damaged variant from the existing cracked geometry, whose editable geometry source is `source/CrackedOozingTube.blend`. It preserves the cut opening, fracture branches, hanging strands, ground-bound flow and floor pool exactly, then changes the core and glass to cyan and the exposed fluid/residue to saturated blue. It also creates the embedded-map Studio copy through `tools/build_bluetube_studio_textured.py`. The static spill is part of the mesh; no fluid simulation is implied.
