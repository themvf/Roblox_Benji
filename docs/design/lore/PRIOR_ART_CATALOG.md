# Prior Art Catalog: asteroid and quantum stories

> Companion to the [lore bible](DARK_MATTER_LORE_BIBLE.md). Compiled 2026-11-16.
>
> Purpose: know what already exists so *The Last Crossing* doesn't retell it.
>
> Limits: no catalog of these two genres can be complete. This covers the well-known and the
> closest-matching works, about 120 titles. It was compiled mainly from general knowledge, with web
> searches to pick up recent releases. It is a creative reference, not a legal clearance. Names
> flagged in section 3 should get a proper trademark check before launch.

---

## 1. Bottom line

- **No single work found combines our three pillars:** an asteroid countdown, a quantum escape to
  another reality, and several factions fighting over a handful of seats.
- **Each pillar on its own is well-worn.** "Who gets a seat when the world ends" is the most used of
  the three. It has been done with rockets, bunkers, arks and caves since 1951.
- **Our escape route is what's new.** Everyone else leaves by rocket or hides underground. Nobody
  catalogued here escapes an asteroid by leaving for a reality where it never came.
- **Three names collide with famous works** and are worth changing: *Wraith*, *Colossus* and the
  *Dark Matter* title (section 3).

## 2. The closest overlaps

These are the works a reviewer or player is most likely to compare us to.

| Work | What it shares with us | How we stay different |
|---|---|---|
| **When Worlds Collide** (film, 1951) | A doomed Earth, one escape craft, seats by lottery, and a rich backer who demands a seat for funding it. | This is the original of our whole "seats" plot. Ours escapes sideways into another reality, not to another planet. |
| **Deep Impact** (film, 1998) | A national lottery for shelter places, plus a pre-selected list of useful people. | Our Government "List" and the Forge's lottery are this same argument. Our finale ending on a lottery is the most familiar beat we have. |
| **2012** (film, 2009) | Secret arks, with seats sold to billionaires for a fortune. | Kade selling seats is this idea. Keep her interesting through the pendant and the Observer, not the sale itself. |
| **Greenland** (2020) and **Greenland 2: Migration** (2026) | A comet, and government-selected families racing to bunkers. | The most recent film in the space. Ours is squads fighting, not a family road trip. |
| **Don't Look Up** (film, 2021) | A comet, a tech billionaire who wrecks the response, and the elite fleeing on a private ship. | Tone. That film is satire. Ours is adventure. |
| **Salvation** (TV, 2017-18) | An asteroid with a day countdown, a tech billionaire, a government cover-up and a secret ark. | Very close on the billionaire-versus-government axis. It has no quantum or other-reality element. |
| **Tomorrowland** (film, 2015) | A doomsday countdown, and a hidden other dimension reserved for a chosen elite. | The nearest film to "escape to another reality while Earth ends". No asteroid and no fight over seats. |
| **Coherence** (film, 2013) | A passing comet causes realities to overlap, and people meet other versions of themselves. | The only work found that joins a comet to quantum overlap. It is a small dinner-party thriller. It also shares a word with our Coherence Institute. |
| **Marvel: Incursions and Secret Wars** (comics, 2013-15) | Realities collide and die, and a "life raft" carries a hand-picked few to safety. | Closest comic to "a small Manifest survives the end". Superheroes, no asteroid. |
| **DC: Convergence** (comics, 2015) | Cities from doomed realities are forced to fight each other to survive. | Shares our game's name and the "fight to exist" premise. See section 3. |
| **Valorant** (game, 2020) | Mirrored teams are explained by a parallel Earth: you fight another reality's version of your side. | Uses another reality to justify mirror matches, as we do. No doomsday clock. |
| **Marvel Rivals** (game, 2024) | A hero shooter whose premise is realities colliding after an experiment goes wrong. | Same "realities converge, so heroes fight" excuse. No asteroid, no seats. |
| **Fortnite** (game, 2017 onward) | A meteor event, realities merging, and a device at the centre of all realities. | The reference point our audience knows best. Ours has one clear story and a countdown. |
| **Dark Matter** (novel 2016, TV 2024) | A machine that lets a person step into other realities, and the question of which "you" is real. | Shares our "are you still you" fear, and our v1 title. See section 3. |
| **Devs** (TV, 2020) | A secret, giant quantum computer and the many-worlds idea. | The closest thing to the Lattice. A slow thriller with no action. |
| **The Alters** (game, 2025) | A quantum computer creates alternate versions of one person. | Close to our Echoes. A survival game, not a shooter. |
| **Rage** (game, 2011; comic *After the Impact*) | An asteroid hits, and chosen people survive in buried "Arks". | Set after the impact. Ours is set before it. |

## 3. Name collisions

| Our name | Collides with | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Wraith** (Kestrel's mutation) | *Apex Legends* has a famous character named Wraith who phases between dimensions and comes from another reality. Ours has the same name and nearly the same power. | Rename. This is the strongest collision in the bible. |
| **Colossus** (Titan's mutation) | A major Marvel X-Men character, also a big armoured strongman. | Rename, or get a trademark check. |
| **Dark Matter** (v1 title, still the filename) | Blake Crouch's novel and the 2024 Apple TV series about crossing realities; also an unrelated 2015 TV series and its comic. | Already retired as the title in v2. Avoid bringing it back as a product name. |
| **Convergence** (the mode and game title) | DC's 2015 comic event *Convergence*; "the Convergence" in *Thor: The Dark World*. | A common English word, widely used. Probably fine as a mode name; check before using it alone as the game's title. |
| **The Observer** | The Observers in *Fringe*, bald watchers from another timeline. Marvel also has the Watcher. | Lower risk, since ours is one person with a different look. Keep, or consider a more personal name tied to Marsh. |
| **Coherence Institute** | The film *Coherence* (comet plus overlapping realities). | Low risk. It is a real physics term. |
| **Titan** | *Titanfall* and many others. | Generic. Fine. |
| **Echo / Echoes** | Characters in Marvel and *Overwatch*. | Generic as a word for a copy. Fine. |
| **Manifest**, **the Crossing** | Two unrelated TV series (2018). | No real overlap. Fine. |

## 5b. Bunkers and shelters

Bunkers are a shared trope, not one franchise's property. What matters is which bunker story ours
would resemble.

| Work | Year | Its bunker idea |
|---|---|---|
| Dr. Strangelove | 1964 | Leaders plan to ride out doomsday in mineshafts with a hand-picked population. |
| Fallout (games, TV) | 1997 on, 2024 on | A corporation sells places in numbered Vaults that are secretly social experiments; the story begins when a dweller walks out into the wasteland generations later. Cheerful 1950s styling. |
| Blast from the Past | 1999 | A family spends 35 years in a shelter by mistake. |
| City of Ember | 2003 book, 2008 film | An underground city whose builders' instructions were lost. |
| Metro 2033 | 2005 book, 2010 game | Survivors live as rival factions in the Moscow subway. |
| The 100 | 2014-20 | A mountain bunker whose residents cannot leave. |
| 10 Cloverfield Lane | 2016 | Three people in one man's shelter; is the danger outside real? |
| Silo (Wool) | 2011 books, 2023 on TV | Thousands live in a buried tower and are forbidden to ask why. |
| Paradise | 2025 | A whole town inside a mountain, built with billionaire money, run as a quiet dictatorship. |
| Greenland, Deep Impact, 2012, Rage | various | Covered above: bunkers and arks as the prize people race toward. |

**What makes something read as Fallout** is the package, not the bunker: a corporation selling the
shelters, numbered vaults, experiments on the residents, retro advertising and a mascot, matching
jumpsuits, a giant gear-shaped door, and a story set long *after* the disaster.

**How ours differs, and should keep differing:**

1. **Ours is set before the impact.** Fallout, Silo, Metro and Paradise are all about life after.
   We never need a wasteland or a "first step outside" scene.
2. **Bunkers are the plan that won't work.** The Rock is extinction-level. Everyone in our world
   knows a bunker only buys time, which is exactly why the Lattice is worth fighting over.
3. **A bunker is a departure lounge, not a home.** People wait there for a seat. That makes it a
   place of queues, lists and tension, with nobody settling in for 200 years.
4. **Bunkers sit on Convergence Points.** The same bunker can exist flooded, frozen or already
   broken open in a neighbouring reality. No other bunker story can do that.
5. **Avoid the Fallout vocabulary and look.** Never "Vault", never numbered, no mascot, no retro
   ads, no jumpsuits, no experiments on residents. Suggested word: **Holds** (as in a ship's hold,
   and "holding for a seat").

The closest live comparison for billionaire and government bunkers is *Paradise*, not Fallout.
Keep Kade's bunkers as a backdrop and a bluff, never as a society we explore.

## 6. What is still open ground

These parts of *The Last Crossing* had no close match in anything catalogued:

1. **Escaping an asteroid sideways.** Leaving for a reality where it never came, not by rocket or bunker.
2. **The 10-15 seat Manifest.** A hard cap that is also the squad size. Other stories save thousands
   or one family.
3. **Four sympathetic sides.** Most stories have a hero and a villain. Ours has a government, the
   rich, criminals and a revolt, each partly right.
4. **Criminals with a code.** The Ferrymen as honest thieves running a black market in seats.
5. **Echoes as the cost.** Copies left behind by bad jumps, and the fear that a crosser is one.
6. **A villain who wants nobody to leave.** The Observer wants an empty reality, not power.
7. **A countdown that runs across real seasons.** *Majora's Mask* has the clock, but inside one game.

## 7. What to steer away from

- **The lottery as the big finale.** It is the most-used ending in the genre (*When Worlds Collide*,
  *Deep Impact*, *The Wandering Earth*). The Issue #6 ending currently lands on it. Consider a twist
  specific to our universe, such as the Lattice itself choosing.
- **"Blow up the asteroid" plots.** That is *Armageddon* and most TV movies. Our Rock should stay
  unstoppable so the story never becomes a deflection mission.
- **A rocket ark for the rich as the main event.** That is *2012* and *Don't Look Up*. Keep Kade's
  rockets as a backup detail.
- **Meeting your own double as the central hook.** *Dark Matter*, *Counterpart*, *Coherence* and
  *The One* all live there. Our Season 2 seed uses it, so it needs a fresh angle.
- **The names Wraith and Colossus** (section 3).
