# CONVERGENCE: The Dark Matter Saga — Lore Bible v1

> Status: creative draft for review. The display renames in section 12 are now in the game
> (text only, no gameplay change). Everything else is still a proposal.

---

## 1. The pitch, in one breath

Eighty-five percent of the matter in the universe is invisible. Scientists call it **dark matter**. We
know it is there only because it pulls on everything around it.

In **2089** a team of engineers finished **the Lattice**, a quantum computer the size of a city,
cooled almost to absolute zero beneath the polar ice. It was built for one job: simulate dark matter
and find out what it really is.

The first time the Lattice ran, it got an answer. Dark matter isn't a mystery particle. **It is the
weight of every other version of reality**, pressing against ours from next door.

That answer broke something. Our universe started to **fork**. Places on Earth now exist in
several states at once. A shipyard is a shipyard, a frozen ruin and a flooded wreck, all on top of each other.
These overlaps are called **Convergence Points**. When one collapses, only one version survives, and
whoever is standing inside it decides which one.

Two crews of engineers, mechanics and coders race to every Convergence. Each fights to save the
world they believe in. Something that lives *inside* the Lattice wants every Convergence to collapse
into nothing at all.

**Tagline:** *Every reality wants to be the real one. You decide which.*

## 2. Why this lore fits the game we already have

The best lore explains the rules players already feel. Every core Convergence mechanic gets a
reason to exist in the story:

| What the game does | What the story says is happening |
|---|---|
| Three capture zones close 3 → 2 → 1 | A Convergence is a superposition **collapsing**. Each phase, one possible reality "decoheres" and blinks out. The last zone is the moment of collapse. |
| Holding a zone scores points | Your team is **observing** the zone. In quantum physics, measuring something forces it to pick a state, and the more your crew observes it, the more the collapse leans your way. |
| Contested zones freeze | Two observers measuring at once cancel each other out. The zone **flickers** and nobody's reality wins. |
| You respawn after 5 s with 2 s protection | Every crew member carries a **Q-Anchor**, a backup of their quantum state. When you fall, the Anchor re-instantiates you. The 2 s shield is the **decoherence haze** while your atoms finish agreeing on where they are. |
| Mutation energy fills from kills, captures and defending | You absorb **Dark Charge**, leaked dark matter from collapsing branches. At 100 your suit can't hold it any more. |
| Titan mutates into Colossus | Titan's exo-frame vents the Dark Charge into its core and becomes **Colossus** for 25 s, swollen and glowing, borrowing mass from a heavier universe. |
| Bounties mark a hot player | A player who racks up kills or captures becomes **Entangled**. They now matter so much to the outcome that every branch can see them, and they show up on everyone's scanners. |
| Streak tiers (Heating Up → Mythic) | **Resonance.** The longer you survive across collapses, the more realities "agree" you exist. |
| Bots fill empty slots | **Echoes**: imperfect copies of real fighters, pulled in from other branches by the collapse. They're almost right and a little off, which is why they move the way bots do. |
| Lobby → queue → map vote | The **Junction** station picks the next Convergence. Voting is the crew deciding which fracture to jump to first. |
| Duel mode | **Proving Chamber** bouts: controlled, tiny Convergences that crews use to train and to settle arguments. |

The rule we keep: **the story explains the rules; it never changes them.** Same principle as skins.

## 3. The science underneath (simplified and kept honest)

The fiction stretches real ideas, and the real ideas are cool enough to teach. Each one becomes a
collectible **Science Byte** (see §9) that a player can read in the lobby.

- **Dark matter** — about 85% of all matter. It gives off no light, so we "see" it only by how its
  gravity bends galaxies and light. *Fiction stretch:* it is the gravity of neighbouring realities.
- **Qubit** — a quantum bit. A normal bit is 0 *or* 1, but a qubit can be both until it is measured.
  *Fiction:* Convergence Points are qubits the size of a battlefield.
- **Superposition** — being in several states at once. *Fiction:* three zones = three possible worlds.
- **Measurement / collapse** — looking at a quantum system forces it to choose. *Fiction:* holding a
  zone is "looking hard."
- **Entanglement** — two particles linked so measuring one tells you about the other, at any distance.
  *Fiction:* bounty targets, and the link between the hero Marsh and his dark twin.
- **Decoherence** — quantum states fall apart when they touch the noisy outside world. That's why real
  quantum computers are kept colder than outer space. *Fiction:* why the Lattice sits under polar ice,
  and why Snow Fortress matters.
- **Engineering** — torque, gyroscopes, heat sinks, hydraulics, cryogenics. *Fiction:* the Red crew
  builds the machines that survive in a reality that keeps changing its mind.

## 4. The world

### The Fracture (Year Zero)
On the night the Lattice first ran, its lead designer **Professor Halden Marsh** tried to pull the
plug. He was standing at the core when it went critical. He didn't die. He **split**. One Marsh is a
flickering ghost who wants to fix what he made. The other is the thing the crews now call
**the Observer**.

### The two crews (Red and Blue)
Teams are mirrored for fairness, so **both crews are heroes**. They are rivals, not good versus evil.
They want the same thing, a world that survives, and they disagree hard about how to get it.

#### RED — **The Forge Union** *(mechanics and engineers)*
- **Belief:** "Reality is a machine. Machines can be fixed." They want to **stabilise** the
  surviving world: weld it shut, build anchors, make it solid and keep it.
- **Style:** heat, rivets, hydraulic pistons, glowing heat sinks, orange-red warning lights, welding
  sparks. They name their weapons like tools.
- **Home:** the decks of the carrier **UNS Meridian** (our Carrier map) and its dry-docks.
- **Motto:** *"Built to hold."*

#### BLUE — **The Coherence Institute** *(quantum coders and physicists)*
- **Belief:** "Reality is a calculation. Calculations can be corrected." They want to **steer** each
  collapse toward the *best* branch, even when that means letting a familiar world go.
- **Style:** cold light, floating holo-glyphs, clean white armour, frost vents, cyan-blue circuitry.
  They name their weapons like algorithms.
- **Home:** **Hollowpoint Station**, the polar facility above the Lattice (our Snow Fortress map).
- **Motto:** *"Measure twice. Collapse once."*

**The tension that drives the story:** the Forge wants to save *this* world. The Institute wants to
save *the best* world. Sometimes those are different worlds, and then the crews fight.

### The third side: **The Null Choir** *(antagonists)*
The Observer's servants: drones, hijacked mechs and corrupted Echoes. The Choir doesn't want Red *or*
Blue to win a collapse. It wants every Convergence to fall to **null**, a reality with nobody in it to
observe anything. A universe with no observers is perfectly quiet and perfectly still. To the Observer,
that is perfect.

## 5. Protagonists

### Wren "Patch" Calloway — Forge Union (Red)
- **Age 15.** Grew up below decks on the Meridian. She fixed the ship's broken elevators with parts
  from three different realities. (Elevators are a known follow-up in the code, too. She'd get it.)
- **Personality:** fast-talking, practical, fearless around machinery, terrible at sitting still.
  Always has a wrench through a belt loop that changes shape depending on which branch she's standing in.
- **Skill:** can repair anything, *including things that don't fully exist yet*.
- **Flaw:** stubborn. She'd rather weld a broken world together than admit it should end.
- **Want vs need:** she *wants* to save the Meridian exactly as it is. She *needs* to learn that
  some things have to change to survive.
- **Signature line:** *"If it's got bolts, I can talk to it."*

### Ozias "Oz" Adeyemi — Coherence Institute (Blue)
- **Age 16.** The youngest coder ever cleared for the Lattice's control room. After the Fracture he
  started seeing **probability ghosts**: faint after-images of what is *about* to happen.
- **Personality:** quiet, precise, dry sense of humour, secretly scared all the time.
- **Skill:** can read a Convergence like code and spot the bug in reality.
- **Flaw:** overthinks. He runs a thousand simulations while Wren is already halfway across the room.
- **Want vs need:** he *wants* the mathematically best outcome. He *needs* to learn that people
  aren't numbers.
- **Signature line:** *"Three outcomes. Two are bad. Let's go find the third."*

**Wren and Oz are the heart of the comic.** They start as rivals on opposite crews. They become the
only two people who know the truth about Marsh, and so the only two who trust each other.

### Sergeant Bram Okafor — **TITAN** (the first Alter Ego)
- **Forge Union heavy engineer.** Wore a load-lifting exo-frame to rebuild the Meridian's flight deck.
  When a Convergence hit, the frame soaked up so much Dark Charge it fused to him.
- **Titan → Colossus:** at full charge he vents it into the frame's core and becomes **Colossus**,
  1.35× bigger, glowing molten orange, briefly heavier than a person should be.
- **Abilities in story terms:**
  - **Ground Slam (E):** dumps borrowed mass into the floor, and the shockwave pushes everyone back.
  - **Brace (F):** locks the frame's gyros and takes the hits head-on.
  - **Charge (C):** a gyro-assisted sprint. Momentum is just mass doing what it's told.
- **Personality:** gentle giant, terrible jokes, the crew's big brother. He's scared of the day the
  frame won't let him turn back.
- **Line:** *"Easy now. I weigh about four universes at the moment."*

### Kestrel — the Phase Runner (planned second Alter Ego)
- **Coherence Institute infiltrator.** Wears a suit that keeps her a few milliseconds *out of
  sync* with reality. (Fits the Ninja Soldiers art set.)
- **Mutation: Kestrel → Wraith.** At full charge she slips into superposition: briefly in two places,
  hard to hit, leaving a decoy afterimage.
- **Personality:** cool, mysterious, speaks in half-sentences, and she's always somewhere you didn't
  expect. Her secret is that she's from a branch that already collapsed. She has no home to go back to.
- **Line:** *"I was never here. Technically."*

### Professor Halden Marsh (the mentor ghost)
- The Lattice's designer, now a **flickering hologram** living in the Junction's systems (the lobby).
  He briefs players, explains the science and feels guilty about all of it.
- He fades in and out mid-sentence, sometimes finishes sentences he hasn't started, and gets
  younger or older depending on the branch.
- **Line:** *"I wanted to know what the universe was made of. I should have asked whether it wanted
  to be known."*

## 6. Antagonists

### THE OBSERVER (the other Halden Marsh)
- **The main villain.** The half of Marsh that stayed inside the Lattice when it went critical. He
  has spent "years" (machine time) watching every possible future and found that every one ends badly.
  His conclusion: **if nothing exists, nothing can go wrong.**
- **Look:** a tall silhouette made of black glass and dark-matter static. In place of a face there is
  a single **lens** that slowly opens. When it looks at you, the world goes grainy, like a bad signal.
- **What makes him scary but not gory:** he doesn't destroy things, he *un-observes* them. Things he
  stops looking at fade like an old photo.
- **What makes him sympathetic:** he isn't evil for fun. He's exhausted and lonely, and he's trying
  to protect everyone from pain in the worst possible way. He is what Oz could become if Oz only
  trusted numbers.
- **Line:** *"I have seen how every story ends. Let me spare you the middle."*

### Vesper Kade — CEO of Kade Dynamics (the human villain)
- Funded the Lattice. Now she's **selling collapses**: whoever pays her gets their preferred branch
  steered into existence. She secretly arms *both* crews to keep the fighting going.
- **Look:** sharp grey suit, a gold **gyroscope pendant** that spins faster when she lies.
- **Role in the story:** the villain you meet first. The comic makes you think she's the big bad.
  She isn't. The Observer is using her, and she finds out too late.
- **Line:** *"Reality is a market, children. I'm simply the first to open a store."*

### The Null Choir (the enemy army)
- **Choir Drones:** floating spheres that hum a single low note. When several hum together, the
  ground starts to fade. (A future map Event.)
- **Hollow Mechs:** Forge mechs whose pilots were un-observed. The suits keep fighting on their own.
  (Fits the Mech Soldiers asset set.)
- **Corrupted Echoes:** what happens when an Echo (a bot) stays in a collapsing branch too long. Same
  shape as a player, but grainy, with a lens where the face should be.

### The Leviathan (the map-scale monster)
- The **Kraken** event on the Carrier becomes **the Gravity Leviathan**: a dark-matter creature from a
  heavier universe, drawn to the Meridian by the Lattice's signal. It isn't evil; it's *hungry for
  mass.* When it surfaces, everything heavy slides toward it. (Lore for the existing Kraken event.)

## 7. The maps as places in the story

| Map | Story name | What it is | Story beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lobby | **The Junction** | A station that exists between branches, the only place every reality agrees on. Marsh's hologram lives here. | Hub: briefings, comics, Science Bytes, Alter Ego kiosk. |
| Carrier | **UNS Meridian** | Forge Union flagship and mobile dry-dock. Its zones read Flight Deck → Hangar → Bridge. | Where Wren grew up; where the Leviathan hunts. |
| Snow Fortress | **Hollowpoint Station** | Polar outpost above the Lattice. Has to stay frozen because heat causes decoherence. | Season 1 finale: the crews fight over whether to shut the Lattice down. |
| Swamp | **The Bogwater Collider** | A particle collider flooded when a Convergence swapped it with a branch where the sea rose. | Where Kestrel's lost branch can still faintly be seen. |
| Forest | **Whisperwood Lens** | A forest where dark matter bends light. Trees appear twice and sounds arrive before they happen. | Oz's probability ghosts are strongest here. |
| Arena (Duel) | **The Proving Chamber** | A tiny, safe, lab-made Convergence for training. | Rival crews settle disputes "by the numbers." |

**Map Vista hooks** (the hero camera on entry): each Vista shows a branch flicker. The map briefly
appears in an alternate state (frozen, flooded, overgrown) and then snaps back to the playable version.
It's one cheap visual effect that sells the whole premise.

## 8. Season 1 storyline: "THE FIRST COLLAPSE" (six comic issues)

Each issue drops alongside a game update and unlocks in the Junction comic kiosk.

### Issue #1 — "Bolts & Bits"
Wren and Oz meet as enemies at a Convergence on the Meridian. They're fighting over the Bridge zone
when the collapse hits, and both get thrown into a pocket of nowhere. There they meet the flickering
Marsh, who tells them the truth: *"I made two of me. One of me wants to end everything."*
**Cliffhanger:** they wake back on their own sides, and nobody believes them.

### Issue #2 — "Echoes"
Wren notices that one of her crewmates blinks a half-second late. He's an Echo. Oz discovers the
Echoes are pouring in because someone is *pulling* them. Introduce Vesper Kade, all smiles and
funding offers. **Cliffhanger:** Kade's pendant spins when she says "trust me."

### Issue #3 — "Heavy" (Titan origin)
Bram Okafor rebuilds the flight deck during a Leviathan surfacing. To save Wren he overloads his frame
and becomes Colossus for the first time. He saves her, and almost doesn't turn back.
**Cliffhanger:** a Hollow Mech watches from the water, and its visor is a lens.

### Issue #4 — "Phase"
A mysterious runner keeps beating both crews to the Bogwater zones. It's Kestrel. Oz tracks her
probability ghost, and she reveals her branch is already gone. Kestrel defects to help the two kids.
**Cliffhanger:** the Null Choir sings for the first time, and an entire zone fades to grey.

### Issue #5 — "The Market"
Wren and Oz expose Kade's scheme on live broadcast across both crews. Kade runs to Hollowpoint to sell
the Lattice itself, but the Observer has been waiting for her. She becomes his first **un-observed**
human, and fades mid-sentence. (Kept eerie, not violent; she might come back.)
**Cliffhanger:** the Observer speaks to everyone at once: *"Hollowpoint. One final collapse."*

### Issue #6 — "Measure Twice" (Season finale)
Every crew converges on Hollowpoint Station. Forge wants to freeze the Lattice forever. The Institute
wants to reprogram it. The Observer wants it to collapse everything to null. Oz realises the
Observer can't be defeated by force, only by being *seen*: if enough people refuse to stop observing
the world, the null branch can't win. Wren builds the amplifier, Oz writes the code, Bram holds the
door as Colossus and Kestrel carries the signal through the Choir.
**Resolution:** the null collapse fails, but the Observer isn't destroyed. He retreats deeper into the
Lattice. Marsh's ghost gets a little more solid.
**Final panel:** Kade's gold pendant, lying in the snow, starts spinning again.

### The in-game event that matches Issue #6
A limited-time Hollowpoint playlist: Choir Drones drift across the map, and **if both teams leave the
final zone empty for too long, it greys out** (a cosmetic version of the "null" threat). Season reward:
a *Measured* title and a frost-and-ember dual-crew skin. Per the skin rules, cosmetic only.

## 11. How players meet the story in the game

Lore nobody sees doesn't count. In order of cost, using systems we already have:

1. **Names and text (cheapest).** Mode card, zone names, phase callouts ("PHASE 2 — A REALITY HAS
   COLLAPSED"), recap card, Mutation prompt ("DARK CHARGE CRITICAL — PRESS Q"), streak and bounty
   labels. All strings, no new systems.
2. **The Junction (lobby).** A Marsh hologram near the Convergence pad gives a short briefing
   (text bubbles, not voice). A **comic kiosk** shows unlocked pages. A **Science Bytes wall** shows
   real-science cards unlocked by playing (e.g. "Capture 10 zones → unlock *Superposition*").
   Lobby edits go through the `Lobby.rbxm` source workflow.
3. **Map Vistas and Events.** Vista shows the branch-flicker reveal (§7). Existing Events get story
   names: the Carrier Kraken becomes the Gravity Leviathan, and the Siren becomes "LEVIATHAN RISING."
4. **Loading / pre-match card.** One rotating line of lore or a Science Byte per match.
5. **Alter Ego kiosk.** Titan's card carries Bram's bio and line. Kestrel appears as "SIGNAL
   DETECTED…" until she ships.
6. **Season events (most expensive).** The Issue #6 Hollowpoint playlist with Choir Drones.

**Readability rule:** story text never sits in the crosshair zone, never blocks a fight and is always
skippable. Lore is the reward, not a toll gate.

## 13. Tone guide for anyone writing more

- **Wonder first, danger second.** The universe is amazing *and* broken.
- **No gore, ever.** People get *un-observed* (fade), *decohere* (glitch) or *re-instantiate*
  (respawn). Danger reads through light, sound and static.
- **Both crews are right about something.** Never write Red or Blue as the bad guys.
- **Science stays honest at the core.** Stretch the idea, never the fact. If a Science Byte says it, it
  should be true.
- **Characters talk like people.** Wren interrupts, Oz qualifies, Bram jokes, Kestrel says too little,
  Marsh says too much.
- **Every mystery pays off within a season.** Leave one thread (the pendant) for the next.
