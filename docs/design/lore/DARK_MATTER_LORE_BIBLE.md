# CONVERGENCE: The Last Crossing — Lore Bible v2

> Status: creative draft for review. v2 merges the asteroid story with the v1 Dark Matter Saga
> (v1 is in git history). The display renames in section 12 are in the game (text only, no gameplay
> change). Everything else is still a proposal.
>
> This is our own sci-fi universe. The rules in section 3 are canon because we say so. Real science
> is there for names, imagery and Science Bytes, never as a limit on the story.

---

## 1. The pitch, in one breath

An extinction-level asteroid is falling toward Earth. Its catalogue name is **Erebus**. Everyone
just calls it **the Rock**. It cannot be stopped.

Billionaires are sealing their bunkers and fuelling their rockets, knowing neither will be enough. Everyone else is looking at one
machine: **the Lattice**, a quantum computer the size of a city, frozen beneath the polar ice. The
Lattice can do one impossible thing. It can move people into **a different reality, one where the
Rock never comes**.

It carries **10 to 15 people at a time**. It has never been properly tested. Nobody knows whether
the people who arrive on the other side are still the people who left.

The first rushed run tore reality open. Places on Earth now exist in several versions at once.
These overlaps are called **Convergence Points**, and they are the only places a jump can be opened.
Whoever holds a Convergence when it collapses gets the seats.

Four sides want those seats: the **Government**, the **Billionaires**, the **Criminals** and a
**People's Revolt**. Something that lives *inside* the Lattice wants nobody to cross at all.

**Tagline:** *Fifteen seats. One way out. Everybody's coming.*

## 2. Why this lore fits the game we already have

The best lore explains the rules players already feel. Every core Convergence mechanic gets a
reason to exist in the story:

| What the game does | What the story says is happening |
|---|---|
| Squads of 10-15 | One jump carries one **Manifest**: the 10-15 people the Lattice can hold stable at once. Your squad is a Manifest. |
| Three capture zones close 3 → 2 → 1 | A Convergence is several realities stacked on one place. Each phase, one of them **collapses** and blinks out. The last zone is where the **Crossing** opens. |
| Holding a zone scores points | Your squad is **observing** the zone. The Lattice locks a jump onto whoever it can see most clearly. The longer you hold, the more the Crossing leans your way. |
| Contested zones freeze | Two Manifests in one window. The Lattice can't tell who to carry, so the zone **flickers** and nobody gets it. |
| You respawn after 5 s with 2 s protection | Every fighter carries a **Q-Anchor**, a pocket-sized piece of jump tech that stores a backup of you. When you fall, the Anchor rebuilds you. The 2 s shield is the **haze** while your atoms finish agreeing on where they are. |
| Mutation energy fills from kills, captures and defending | You absorb **Dark Charge**, the dark matter that leaks out when a reality collapses. At 100 your suit can't hold it any more. |
| Titan mutates into Colossus | Titan's exo-frame vents the Dark Charge into its core and becomes **Colossus** for 25 s, swollen and glowing, borrowing mass from a heavier universe. |
| Bounties mark a hot player | A player who racks up kills or captures becomes **Entangled**. The Lattice has noticed them, and now every scanner on the map can see them too. |
| Streak tiers | **Resonance.** The longer you survive across collapses, the more realities agree that you exist. |
| Bots fill empty slots | **Echoes**: copies that jumps leave behind or drag in from other realities. They're almost right and a little off, which is why they move the way bots do. |
| Lobby → queue → map vote | **The Junction** tracks every open Convergence. Voting is the squad choosing which jump window to race for. |
| Duel mode | **Proving Chamber** bouts: small practice jump rooms where fighters earn a place on a Manifest. |

The rule we keep: **the story explains the rules; it never changes them.** Same principle as skins.

## 3. The rules of our universe

These are canon. Each one is also a collectible **Science Byte** (see §11) with a true fact attached,
so players learn the real idea that inspired it.

- **Dark matter is the weight of other realities.** Most of the universe is invisible because it
  isn't here. It is next door, pressing on us. *Real kernel:* about 85% of all matter is dark matter,
  and we only know it exists by its gravity.
- **The Lattice can see next door.** By weighing dark matter, it finds neighbouring realities and
  picks out the ones where the Rock misses. *Real kernel:* quantum computers work with qubits, which
  can be 0 and 1 at once.
- **A Crossing moves a person by rewriting them.** The Lattice reads everything you are, erases it
  here and writes it there. Nothing is copied, so there is only ever one of you. Probably.
  *Real kernel:* quantum teleportation moves a state and destroys the original.
- **Fifteen is the limit.** A human being is an enormous amount of information. The Lattice can hold
  10 to 15 people steady for one jump. Try for more and everyone arrives scrambled.
- **Convergence Points are the doors.** Since the Fracture, some places exist in several realities at
  once. Only there is the wall thin enough to cross. *Real kernel:* superposition, being in several
  states at once.
- **Observation decides.** A Convergence collapses toward whoever is holding it. *Real kernel:*
  measuring a quantum system forces it to choose.
- **Cold keeps it stable.** Heat and noise shake a jump apart, which is why the Lattice lives under
  the ice. *Real kernel:* decoherence, and why real quantum computers run colder than outer space.
- **The Rock exists in almost every reality.** Safe realities are rare. Finding one takes time the
  world does not have.

**The fear at the centre of the story:** nobody who has crossed has ever come back to report. Is the
person who arrives really you, or just an Echo that thinks it is?

## 4. The world

### The Rock
Erebus came out of the Sun's glare, where no telescope was looking. By the time NASA confirmed the
orbit, there were **412 days** left. Every plan to deflect it failed. The countdown runs through the
whole story, and each comic issue opens with the day count.

### The Holds
Governments and corporations dug bunkers as soon as the orbit was confirmed. People call them
**Holds**, as in a ship's hold, and because everyone inside is holding for a seat.

- **A Hold is a departure lounge, not a home.** Nobody believes a bunker survives the Rock. It only
  buys time. People wait there for their Manifest to be called.
- **What a Hold feels like:** queues, lists on the walls, bags never unpacked, a departures board
  showing jump windows and the day count.
- **Holds sit on Convergence Points,** because that is where a Crossing can open. So the same Hold
  can flicker flooded, frozen or already broken open, as a neighbouring reality shows through.
- **Each side runs them differently.** Institute Holds are orderly and guarded, and you need to be
  on the List. Kade Holds are luxury suites where a seat is a purchase. The Ferrymen run hidden ones
  in drains and wrecks. The Forge breaks Holds open.
- **Style rule:** Holds are named after places, never numbered. They are bare concrete and
  emergency lighting, with no mascots, no cheerful advertising and no uniforms. We never tell a
  story about a society living underground for generations. (See the bunkers section of the
  [prior art catalog](PRIOR_ART_CATALOG.md).)

### The Lattice and the Fracture
The Lattice was a research machine built to study dark matter. When the Rock was confirmed, its lead
designer **Professor Halden Marsh** was ordered to turn it into an escape route. He said it wasn't
ready. They ran it anyway.

That first run is called **the Fracture**. Reality forked. Convergence Points opened across the
planet. Marsh was standing at the core and he **split**. One Marsh is a flickering ghost who wants to
get everyone out safely. The other is the thing people now call **the Observer**.

### The four sides
Nobody here is simply evil. Each side has a fair point and a blind spot. All four are playable.

#### THE FORGE UNION — *the People's Revolt*
- **Who:** mechanics, dock workers, engineers, pilots. The people who built the machines and were
  left off the list.
- **Belief:** "Seats by lottery, not by wallet." Many of them also believe the world can still be
  saved, and that running is giving up.
- **Style:** heat, rivets, hydraulic pistons, welding sparks, orange-red warning lights, hand-painted
  banners. They name their weapons like tools.
- **Home:** the seized carrier **UNS Meridian**.
- **Motto:** *"Built to hold."*

#### THE COHERENCE INSTITUTE — *the Government*
- **Who:** the official evacuation program. Soldiers, physicists, coders.
- **Belief:** "Save the ones who can rebuild." They keep **the List**, the people chosen to cross
  first. They call it necessary. Everyone else calls it unfair.
- **Style:** cold light, floating holo-glyphs, clean white armour, frost vents, cyan-blue circuitry.
  They name their weapons like algorithms.
- **Home:** **Hollowpoint Station**, the polar base above the Lattice.
- **Motto:** *"Measure twice. Cross once."*

#### KADE DYNAMICS — *the Billionaires*
- **Who:** the private money that paid for the Lattice, plus the security army it hired.
- **Belief:** "We paid for it. We own it." They have rockets and luxury Holds as a backup, and they
  sell seats to anyone who can afford one.
- **Style:** sharp grey and gold, polished chrome, private logos on everything, drones that look like
  jewellery.
- **Home:** a private launch complex (see §7).
- **Motto:** *"Reality is a market."*

#### THE FERRYMEN — *the Criminals*
- **Who:** smugglers, hackers, salvage crews and thieves, organised into one syndicate.
- **Belief:** "Nobody was ever going to save us, so we're saving ourselves." They steal jump tech,
  build their own machines out of scrap and run a black market in seats.
- **Style:** patched-together gear, neon tape, stolen parts from the other three sides, coin motifs.
- **Home:** the drowned labs of **the Bogwater Collider**.
- **Motto:** *"Everyone crosses. Everyone pays."*

**The tension that drives the story:** there are far more people than seats. Every side has a
different answer to "who goes?", and none of the answers is good enough.

### The fifth side: **The Null Choir** *(antagonists)*
The Observer's servants: drones, hijacked mechs and corrupted Echoes. The Choir doesn't want anyone
to win a Convergence. It wants every one of them to collapse to **null**, a reality with nobody in it.

### How four sides fit a two-team game
Matches stay Red versus Blue. Teams are mirrored and colour is the primary signal. The faction is a
layer on top: each match pairs two of the four, or a player pledges to one as a cosmetic allegiance.
Which of these we build is an open question (see the end).

## 5. Protagonists

### Wren "Patch" Calloway — Forge Union
- **Age 15.** Grew up below decks on the Meridian. Her family's names were never on the List.
- **Personality:** fast-talking, practical, fearless around machinery, terrible at sitting still.
  Always has a wrench through a belt loop that changes shape depending on which reality she's standing in.
- **Skill:** can repair anything, *including things that don't fully exist yet*.
- **Flaw:** stubborn. She'd rather weld a broken world together than admit it might have to be left.
- **Want vs need:** she *wants* to save the world she has. She *needs* to learn that getting people
  out is not the same as giving up.
- **Signature line:** *"If it's got bolts, I can talk to it."*

### Ozias "Oz" Adeyemi — Coherence Institute
- **Age 16.** The youngest coder ever cleared for the Lattice's control room. After the Fracture he
  started seeing **probability ghosts**: faint after-images of what is *about* to happen. He has
  also seen the List, and his own name is on it.
- **Personality:** quiet, precise, dry sense of humour, secretly scared all the time.
- **Skill:** can read a Convergence like code and spot the bug in reality.
- **Flaw:** overthinks. He runs a thousand simulations while Wren is already halfway across the room.
- **Want vs need:** he *wants* the mathematically best outcome. He *needs* to learn that people
  aren't numbers.
- **Signature line:** *"Three outcomes. Two are bad. Let's go find the third."*

**Wren and Oz are the heart of the comic.** They start as enemies on opposite sides. They become the
only two people who know the truth about Marsh, and so the only two who trust each other.

### Sergeant Bram Okafor — **TITAN** (the first Alter Ego)
- **Forge Union heavy engineer.** Wore a load-lifting exo-frame to rebuild the Meridian's flight deck.
  When a Convergence hit, the frame soaked up so much Dark Charge it fused to him.
- **Titan → Colossus:** at full charge he vents it into the frame's core and becomes **Colossus**,
  1.35× bigger, glowing molten orange, briefly heavier than a person should be.
- **Abilities in story terms:**
  - **Ground Slam (E):** dumps borrowed mass into the floor, and the shockwave pushes everyone back.
  - **Brace (F):** locks the frame's gyros and takes the hits head-on.
  - **Charge (C):** a gyro-assisted sprint. Momentum is just mass doing what it's told.
- **Personality:** gentle giant, terrible jokes, the crew's big brother. He's scared of the day the
  frame won't let him turn back.
- **Line:** *"Easy now. I weigh about four universes at the moment."*

### Kestrel — the Phase Runner (planned second Alter Ego)
- **The Ferrymen's best runner.** Wears a stolen suit that keeps her a few milliseconds *out of sync*
  with reality. (Fits the Ninja Soldiers art set.)
- **Mutation: Kestrel → Wraith.** At full charge she is briefly in two places at once, hard to hit,
  leaving a decoy afterimage.
- **Personality:** cool, mysterious, speaks in half-sentences, always somewhere you didn't expect.
- **Her secret:** she has already crossed once. She came *from* another reality, one where the Rock
  hit. She is the only proof that a Crossing works, and she isn't sure she's still herself.
- **Line:** *"I was never here. Technically."*

### Professor Halden Marsh (the mentor ghost)
- The Lattice's designer, now a **flickering hologram** living in the Junction's systems (the lobby).
  He briefs players, explains the science and feels guilty about all of it.
- He fades in and out mid-sentence, sometimes finishes sentences he hasn't started, and gets
  younger or older depending on the reality.
- **Line:** *"They asked me for a door. I told them I'd only built a window."*

## 6. Antagonists

### THE OBSERVER (the other Halden Marsh)
- **The main villain.** The half of Marsh that stayed inside the Lattice. He has looked into every
  reality the machine can reach, and the Rock is in almost all of them. In the few that are safe,
  something else goes wrong later. His conclusion: **if nothing exists, nothing can be lost.**
- **Look:** a tall silhouette made of black glass and dark-matter static. In place of a face there is
  a single **lens** that slowly opens. When it looks at you, the world goes grainy, like a bad signal.
- **What makes him scary but not gory:** he doesn't destroy things, he *un-observes* them. Things he
  stops looking at fade like an old photo.
- **What makes him sympathetic:** he isn't evil for fun. He's exhausted and lonely, and he's trying
  to spare everyone the fear of the countdown in the worst possible way. He is what Oz could become
  if Oz only trusted numbers.
- **Line:** *"I have seen how every story ends. Let me spare you the middle."*

### Vesper Kade — CEO of Kade Dynamics (the human villain)
- Funded the Lattice and forced the first run. Now she **sells seats**, and she secretly arms every
  side to keep the price climbing.
- **Look:** sharp grey suit, a gold **gyroscope pendant** that spins faster when she lies.
- **Role in the story:** the villain you meet first. The comic makes you think she's the big bad.
  She isn't. The Observer is using her, and she finds out too late.
- **Line:** *"Reality is a market, children. I'm simply the first to open a store."*

### Marisol "Ledger" Vance — boss of the Ferrymen (the rival)
- Keeps a handwritten book of everyone who owes her a seat and everyone she owes one to. She has
  never broken a deal, which is more than the Government or Kade can say.
- **Look:** long coat lined with stolen ID badges, a brass coin she flips before every decision.
- **Role in the story:** a thief with a code. An enemy in Issue #4, an uneasy ally by the finale.
- **Line:** *"I don't decide who deserves to live. I just check who paid."*

### The Null Choir (the enemy army)
- **Choir Drones:** floating spheres that hum a single low note. When several hum together, the
  ground starts to fade. (A future map Event.)
- **Hollow Mechs:** mechs whose pilots were un-observed. The suits keep fighting on their own.
  (Fits the Mech Soldiers asset set.)
- **Corrupted Echoes:** what happens when an Echo (a bot) stays in a collapsing reality too long. Same
  shape as a player, but grainy, with a lens where the face should be.

### The Leviathan (the map-scale monster)
- The **Kraken** event on the Carrier is **the Gravity Leviathan**: a dark-matter creature from a
  heavier universe, pulled through by the Meridian's stolen jump core. It isn't evil; it's *hungry
  for mass.* When it surfaces, everything heavy slides toward it.

## 7. The locations

Each map is a set-piece in the story, the way a film moves between places.

| Map | Location | What it is | The scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lobby | **The Junction** | A station that sits between realities, the one place they all agree on. Marsh's hologram lives here. | The staging room. Squads wait here for the next jump window. |
| Snow Fortress | **Hollowpoint Station** | The polar base built over the Lattice. Government stronghold. | The final act: all four sides arrive at once. |
| Carrier | **UNS Meridian** | A warship turned evacuation fleet, seized by the Forge Union. It carries a stolen jump core. | Where Wren grew up; where the Leviathan hunts. |
| Swamp | **The Bogwater Collider** | A prototype lab that a Convergence swapped with a flooded reality. | The Ferrymen strip it for parts to build their own machine. |
| Forest | **Whisperwood** | A test site where a jump went wrong. Trees appear twice and sounds arrive before they happen. | Oz's probability ghosts are strongest here. |
| Arena (Duel) | **The Proving Chamber** | A small, safe, lab-made Convergence. | Fighters earn their place on a Manifest. |

**Newer maps, not yet placed.** These are first suggestions to confirm against each map's layout:

| Map | Suggested location |
|---|---|
| Five Islands | **Kade's Archipelago**: the Billionaires' private launch complex, rockets on the pads and a hurricane rolling in. |
| Sci-Fi Foundry | **The Core Foundry**: the plant where jump cores are cast. Every side needs what it makes. |
| Choke Ridge | **The Ridge Road**: the one mountain pass convoys must take to reach Hollowpoint. |
| Snow Forest | **The Treeline**: the frozen woods outside Hollowpoint's perimeter, where people without a seat have made camp. |
| MyMap (public arena) | Unplaced. |

**Holds as set dressing.** Any map can contain a Hold entrance or interior (see §4): a blast door,
a waiting hall, a departures board. It is a cheap way to show ordinary people in the story without
adding characters.

**Map Vista hooks** (the hero camera on entry): each Vista shows a reality flicker. The map briefly
appears in another version (frozen, flooded, burning under the Rock) and then snaps back to the
playable one. It's one cheap visual effect that sells the whole premise.

## 8. Season 1 storyline: "THE FIRST CROSSING" (six comic issues)

Each issue drops alongside a game update and unlocks in the Junction comic kiosk. Each opens with the
day count.

### Issue #1 — "Bolts & Bits" *(Day 412)*
Wren and Oz meet as enemies at a Convergence on the Meridian. They're fighting over the Bridge zone
when the collapse hits, and both get thrown into a pocket of nowhere. There they meet the flickering
Marsh, who tells them the truth: *"I made two of me. One of me wants nobody to leave."*
**Cliffhanger:** they wake back on their own sides, and nobody believes them.

### Issue #2 — "Echoes" *(Day 371)*
Wren notices that one of her crewmates blinks a half-second late. He's an Echo, left behind by a
jump that went wrong. Oz finds the List, and finds his own name on it. Introduce Vesper Kade, all
smiles and funding offers. **Cliffhanger:** Kade's pendant spins when she says "trust me."

### Issue #3 — "Heavy" (Titan origin) *(Day 318)*
Bram Okafor rebuilds the flight deck during a Leviathan surfacing. To save Wren he overloads his frame
and becomes Colossus for the first time. He saves her, and almost doesn't turn back.
**Cliffhanger:** a Hollow Mech watches from the water, and its visor is a lens.

### Issue #4 — "Phase" *(Day 240)*
A mysterious runner keeps beating everyone to the Bogwater zones. It's Kestrel, working for Ledger
Vance and the Ferrymen. Oz tracks her probability ghost, and she admits she has already crossed
once, from a reality the Rock destroyed. A Crossing works. She's the proof.
**Cliffhanger:** the Null Choir sings for the first time, and an entire zone fades to grey.

### Issue #5 — "The Market" *(Day 127)*
Wren and Oz expose Kade's seat-selling on live broadcast to all four sides. Kade runs to Hollowpoint
to take the Lattice for herself, but the Observer has been waiting for her. She becomes his first
**un-observed** human, and fades mid-sentence. (Kept eerie, not violent; she might come back.)
**Cliffhanger:** the Observer speaks to everyone at once: *"Hollowpoint. One final collapse."*

### Issue #6 — "Measure Twice" (Season finale) *(Day 60)*
All four sides converge on Hollowpoint. The Government wants the List to cross. Kade's board wants
the machine. The Ferrymen want the core. The Forge wants a lottery. The Observer wants it all to
collapse to null. Oz realises the Observer can't be beaten by force, only by being *seen*: if enough
people refuse to stop observing the world, the null reality can't win. Wren builds the amplifier, Oz
writes the code, Bram holds the door as Colossus, Kestrel carries the signal through the Choir and
Ledger Vance, for once, does a job for free.
**Resolution:** the null collapse fails, and the first true Crossing opens. Fifteen seats. The four
sides, standing in the same room, agree to draw lots. The Observer retreats deeper into the Lattice.
Marsh's ghost gets a little more solid.
**Final panel:** Kade's gold pendant, lying in the snow, starts spinning again. Caption: *Day 59.*

### The in-game event that matches Issue #6
A limited-time Hollowpoint playlist: Choir Drones drift across the map, and **if both teams leave the
final zone empty for too long, it greys out** (a cosmetic version of the "null" threat). Season reward:
a *First Manifest* title and a four-faction skin. Per the skin rules, cosmetic only.

## 11. How players meet the story in the game

Lore nobody sees doesn't count. In order of cost, using systems we already have:

1. **Names and text (cheapest).** Mode card, zone names, phase callouts ("PHASE 2 — A REALITY HAS
   COLLAPSED"), win banner ("CROSSING LOCKED"), recap card, Mutation prompt, streak and bounty labels.
   All strings, no new systems.
2. **The countdown.** One line on the loading card or lobby wall: "DAY 412". It ticks down with each

## 13. Tone guide for anyone writing more

- **Comic book, not textbook.** State the universe's rules with confidence. Never stop the story to
  explain why something couldn't really happen.
- **Wonder first, danger second.** The end of the world is frightening, and the way out is amazing.
- **No gore, ever.** People get *un-observed* (fade), *decohere* (glitch) or are *rebuilt* (respawn).
  Danger reads through light, sound and static. The Rock is a countdown, not a body count.
- **Every side is right about something.** The Government is organised, the Billionaires paid for
  it, the Ferrymen keep their word and the Forge is fair. None is the bad guy.
- **Criminals are thieves and smugglers, not killers.** Heist-movie energy.
- **Before, never after.** The story is the countdown. No wasteland, no survivors emerging from
  underground. Holds are waiting rooms, not vaults.
- **Science Bytes stay true.** The story can do anything. A card that says "this is real" has to be.
- **Characters talk like people.** Wren interrupts, Oz qualifies, Bram jokes, Kestrel says too little,
  Marsh says too much, Ledger counts.
- **Every mystery pays off within a season.** Leave one thread (the pendant) for the next.

- **The Satellite Array**: a map built around the telescope that first saw the Rock (we already have
  satellite dish assets).
