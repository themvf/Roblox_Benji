Four other ways to build the hub

Each keeps exactly what the fifth version holds: five arrival spawns, the same five queue pads at their gameplay sizes, an ARMORY, a KITS hall, a SHOP, the leaderboard, and the lobby's own canister, stone columns and purple columns. Floor area and wall height are about the same. What changes is how the pieces are arranged around the player, and what you see in the first five seconds.

Blockout renders from Blender, built from the same parts as the concept page, with no roofs. They show layout, scale and sightlines, not final art or Roblox lighting. Walk times are straight-line distance at Roblox's default walk speed. None of these is built; the fifth version is what is in the lobby file now.

Option A

Orbit

The PLAY gate stands on a dais in the middle of one big round atrium. The pads ring the dais, ARMORY and KITS open off the atrium wall left and right, and the SHOP is behind you.

What it does well

  • Shortest walk to a match: Convergence is 3 s away, the gate 5 s.
  • The anomaly is the centre of the room, so it is in view from almost everywhere, including the hall doors.
  • Compact, and built from the same round walls and halls the fifth version uses.

What to watch

  • On arrival the gate fills the view. It is dramatic, but you cannot take in the rest of the room without turning.
  • Duel and TDM sit just outside the first view, about 55 degrees to each side.
  • ARMORY and KITS are the furthest of any layout (about 12 s), because they sit outside the ring.
  • Pads on a circle are harder to scan than pads in a row.

Option B

Overlook

You arrive on a terrace 12 studs up and look down over the whole PLAY floor: every pad and the gate in one view. A wide central stair and two side stairs lead down. ARMORY and KITS open off the terrace wings; the SHOP is behind you.

What it does well

  • The only layout where all five modes and the gate are visible the moment you spawn, from above, so the pads read as a map rather than a row.
  • Walking down the stairs is a clear moment of committing to play; the terrace stays calm for browsing the armory, kits and shop.
  • Players on the floor are easy to see from the terrace, so the lobby looks busy.
  • The lobby markers already allow it: they check footprint, not height.

What to watch

  • Two levels: the halls and the shop sit on 12-stud platforms, so going from the armory to a pad means walking down a stair.
  • The gate is 13 s away, about as far as in the fifth version.
  • Stairs need a ramp or a gentle slope for players who struggle to climb them on a phone.

Option C

Boulevard

One straight street from the arrival point to the gate court. Every hall is an open storefront off the street, with a blade sign that faces you as you arrive. The leaderboard and canister get their own storefront, a Hall of Fame.

What it does well

  • Simplest to read and to build: one direction, signs that face you, and a new destination later is just another storefront.
  • ARMORY and KITS are the closest of any layout (about 6 s).
  • The pads sit in the street you have to walk down anyway.

What to watch

  • Longest walk to the gate (about 18 s) and to Convergence (16 s).
  • A long straight street with walls is the corridor shape you found claustrophobic in the third version, even at 90 studs wide.
  • Least sense of a central place to stand and look around.

Option D

Islands

Floating platforms over the dark-matter void, joined by railed bridges. From the arrival island you look straight up the bridges to the pad island and the gate island beyond it. ARMORY, KITS and SHOP are islands of their own.

What it does well

  • The most distinctive and the most open: no walls, sky everywhere, and it fits the dark-matter story.
  • The route to play is one straight line of bridges, so nobody gets lost.
  • Each island is a separate piece, easy to add to or move later.

What to watch

  • People can fall off. The lobby has no fall handling today, so it needs invisible walls along every rail and a recovery teleport.
  • Bridges are pinch points when a full server crowds towards PLAY.
  • Heaviest to build and to run: about 860 parts against about 200 for the others, which matters on low-end phones.
  • The void needs its own lighting and sky work to look deep rather than just dark.

Recommendation

Overlook. It is the only layout that answers the question a new player has on arrival, "what can I play?", with one look: every pad and the gate are laid out below you. It keeps the open sky of the fifth version, needs no new systems, and the lobby markers already accept a raised arrival.

Worth combining: the fifth version's plaza with an Overlook arrival. The spawns go on a raised terrace at the back of the plaza, so the first view looks down the avenue over the pads to the gate. That keeps the layout that is already built and changes only the arrival.

Orbit is the quickest to play; Boulevard is the simplest to extend; Islands is the most memorable but needs fall handling and the most work.
